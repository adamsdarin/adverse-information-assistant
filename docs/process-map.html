Adverse Information Assistant — Process Map

A multi-agent assistant that helps clearance holders and applicants prepare complete, candid adverse-information self-reports. The design goal is first-time-right submissions: fewer round-trips with federal agencies.

Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or reviewed by DCSA, DOHA, ODNI, or any U.S. Government agency. The Government makes all adjudicative determinations.  ·  Source diagrams live in PROCESS.md and render natively on GitHub.

1

Session flow

Reporting obligation first, adjudicative criteria second — and the whole thing loops.

A matter rarely arrives alone. An OWI mentions court-ordered therapy, the therapy mentions an assault, the assault mentions cocaine — each separately reportable, each surfaced only because the one before it was explored. Accepted threads re-enter at the top as new matters. Two limits keep that from becoming an interrogation: the detector follows only what the user actually said, and every expansion is consented to before scope widens.

2

Privacy tiers

The user decides their own exposure; the tool holds the line they picked.

Two absolutes no tier relaxes: Social Security numbers and dates of birth are never collected, and organizations are always exempt because the form requires them and they aren't private individuals. The business lookup asks consent every time — searching the venue name is itself a disclosure, and a user who chose HIGH would be badly served by the tool quietly broadcasting it.

3

Where questions come from

Three sources, two levels of obligation.

A minor-in-possession is low severity and still needs the court, the date, the disposition, the fine amount, and whether it was paid in full. Severity buys brevity on context, never on the form. Sensitivity is a separate axis — Section 19's counseling questions are both sensitive and required.

4

Trust gates

The tool is model-agnostic, so the scripts are the control.

No platform enforces that an agent only read /corpus — prompt restrictions are requests, not controls. Assembly is template-driven so a model never touches the disclaimer, the crosswalk, or the STILL REQUIRED list. Verification is a hard stop: exit 1 means do not deliver, regardless of which model wrote the draft.

5

What grounds each agent

Every citation resolves to a file on disk.

Everything ships maintainer_verified: false. Until a human checks a corpus file against its official source, agents treat it as uncitable and reportability degrades to consult-your-security-office. The scaffold is honest about being a scaffold.

6

Case retrieval

Guideline tags in the filename and the index — checked against each other.

Denials and grants are read differently: denials show what absence looks like, grants show what a complete record looks like. Both feed questions. Neither produces a prediction — the corpus is contested SOR cases, a selection-biased sample that says nothing about how ordinary reports resolve.

Two rules carry most of the safety. Reportability never returns “no” — only “yes” or “consult your security office” — because a wrong “no” is a reporting violation while a wrong “yes” costs five minutes. And the user decides their own exposure: the privacy tier is theirs to set, Social Security numbers and dates of birth are collected at no tier, and the tool never looks up a person.
